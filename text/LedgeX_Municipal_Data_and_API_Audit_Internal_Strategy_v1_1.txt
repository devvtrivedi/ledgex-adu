04 / PRODUCT AND MOAT

Sell the reconciled exception, not the lookup

The wedge is a dated, source-linked automated Property File that explains what the record supports, what conflicts and
what cannot be concluded.

 Layer                                         Contains                                                                         Role

 Commodity layer                               Parcels, zoning, code summaries and basic maps                                   Useful inputs; not the moat

 Exception layer                               Permit/geometry conflicts, overlays, missing dependencies, rights blocks         Candidate professional wedge

 Trust layer                                   Provenance, licences, confidence, conflicts, methods and refusals                Required product contract

 Evidence asset                                Versioned sources, structured failures, conclusions and outcomes                 Compounds without manual facts

What compounds

 • Immutable retrieved facts and snapshots with provenance, rights and retrieval time.

 • Versioned rule packs, field definitions and conclusion dependency manifests.

 • System-observed source failures, schema drift, refusals and customer outcomes stored in their proper domains.

 • Request-scoped user assumptions kept outside the property-fact ledger.

    NO MANUAL RESOLUTION ASSET

    Portal readings, analyst judgements and human reconciliation do not become parcel facts and are not a Phase 1 delivery path. A
    non-machine source is a declared gap until a supported integration exists.

07 / LIABILITY ARCHITECTURE

Trust must be visible on the product surface

Every output shows the difference between a retrieved fact, an estimate, a user assumption and a derived conclusion.

 Object                             Required surface                                                            Rule

 Fact                               Value, source, retrieval/effective date, licence, confidence, conflict      Immutable; correction inserts successor

 Estimate                           Method, inputs, version and uncertainty                                     Never presented as record or survey

 User assumption                    Request-scoped scenario input                                               Separate store; expires with request

 Conclusion                         Versioned method and dependency manifest                                    Named refusal if required input missing

Three terminal outcomes

 •    Composed: the required Property File is supported and every offered conclusion resolves.

 •    Partial: minimum core is present, but one or more conclusions are refused by name because a required input is absent, blocked or
      unusable.

 •    Refused: minimum core, jurisdiction, parcel identity, rights or safety conditions prevent a useful file.

 •    A caveat may explain a valid weaker conclusion only when a declared substitution supplies every required input. A caveat alone never
      replaces evidence.

     NO HUMAN ESCALATION

     Customer files are never reviewed, completed or supplemented by a person. Internal QA, golden parcels and rule review remain separate
     from delivery.

    v1.1 INTERPRETATION: PORTAL / PDF / MANUAL DESCRIBES SOURCE FRICTION ONLY - NEVER A HUMAN CUSTOMER-DELIVERY OR FACT-ENTRY PATH.

         The audit tested what an external production system can retrieve without a signed municipal agreement. It
         reviewed official city and county pages, ArcGIS service directories, ArcGIS item configurations, open-data
         catalogs and public permitting documentation. Hidden browser calls were not counted as an API unless the
         city published a stable REST/query or explicit bulk-download contract.
         The method and city grades are preserved from LedgeX_City_API_Audit_2026-07-31.docx, dated 31 July 2026.

           Grade                   Meaning

           A+                      Queryable core GIS plus a useful public permit/planning feed.

           A                       Stable queryable public GIS for parcels/zoning/property context; permits may remain portal-only.

           A*                      Technically strong API, but a material noncommercial or permission restriction blocks immediate product
                                   use.

           B                       Partial, bulk, dated or viewer-backed machine access; not a complete core contract.

           C                       Search/apply portal exists, but no supported public data API for the required facts.

           D                       Primarily PDF, email, counter or manual-record workflow.

         What "API integration" means here
             • Direct: ArcGIS FeatureServer/MapServer or documented open-data endpoint supports query/export in
               JSON, GeoJSON, PBF, CSV or comparable machine format.
             • Bulk: a city publishes downloadable shapefiles/GeoJSON/ZIPs but not a query endpoint. Automatable on
               a schedule, but not a stream.
             • Portal-only: eTRAKiT, Accela, EnerGov, MGO, Cloudpermit, Clariti or a custom viewer provides user
               functionality without a documented public data API.
             • Manual: PDF maps, monthly reports, email/counter research or public-record requests are required.

         Critical limitations
             • "Public" does not guarantee free commercial redistribution, an SLA, a rate limit, or permanent schema
               stability.
             • Parcel geometry, assessor characteristics, ownership, zoning, permits and field conditions have different
               stewards and refresh cycles. A missing documented API means no public production contract was found

             - Grades describe technical and licensing access observed on a date. They are not a judgment about
               market attractiveness, which is addressed in decision memo sections 02-05.

         The Sanborn encumbrance
         LedgeX_City_API_Audit_2026-07-31.docx (31 July 2026) noted that Santa Clara County building footprints derive from
         early-2020 LiDAR and that the layer identifies Sanborn Map Company rights. That source artifact did not treat the issue as a

         It should have been scored as a gate. Building footprint is a load-bearing input to every ADU calculation the
         product performs: existing coverage, remaining buildable area, setback compliance and the buildable
         envelope all depend on it. A third-party rights claim on the core geometric input is a larger commercial
         exposure than any city-level restriction identified elsewhere in this audit — and it sits inside the
         recommended county.
         The precise nature of that claim has not been established. We know only what the layer’s own metadata
         asserts. We have not confirmed who commissioned the acquisition, what rights were conveyed, or what use
         LedgeX would in fact be permitted. That question should be put to the County in writing rather than inferred.

              SANBORN DEPENDENCY REMOVED FROM THE PROPOSED STACK — VALIDATION REMAINS AN
              ENGINEERING GATE
              USGS 3DEP publishes an independent Santa Clara County 2020 lidar dataset, and San Mateo County is
              covered by the Bay Area multi-county project. 3DEP products are stated by USGS to be available free of
              charge and without use restrictions.
              Substituting 3DEP removes the Sanborn layer from the proposed data stack. That is a dependency
              change, not a solved problem.
              The rights position is not fully resolved until LedgeX has successfully extracted footprints from 3DEP,
              validated their accuracy against known structures, and confirmed coverage over the operating area.
              Until all three are demonstrated, treat 3DEP as the intended source and the county layer as unavailable
              — with no fallback assumed.
              When the derived layer ships: label it LedgeX-derived, publish measured error bars, and never present it
              as the County product.

         Why not the free footprint alternatives
         Microsoft Building Footprints, OpenStreetMap and the Overture buildings theme are free and technically
         adequate, but they carry ODbL or ODbL-derived terms. Share-alike obligations on a derived database are a
         plausible threat to a proprietary product database — potentially a worse commercial outcome than the
         Sanborn position being avoided. 3DEP, being free of use restrictions, carries no equivalent obligation. This is
         the deciding factor, not data quality. Confirm the current terms of any source at the moment of use.

         Why not commercial imagery, yet
         Vexcel and Nearmap sell exactly the derived products LedgeX would be asking to produce — building
         footprints and property attributes. Licensing their imagery to compete with their own output is a difficult
         commercial ask. Nearmap’s published terms prohibit using the products to create a database for resale,
         distribution or commercial purposes absent express permission.

         Correct sequencing: match data precision to liability tier. 3DEP-derived geometry with stated error bars for
         screening output, once extraction and accuracy are validated. Commercial imagery or an actual survey only at
         the paid, site-specific tier, where the cost is passed through to the customer.
         When commercial imagery does become relevant, the ask changes. The valuable product is not resolution
         — it is the historical archive. Structure present in the 2023 capture, absent in 2018, with no corresponding
         permit, is the undocumented-structure finding. Historical archive access should therefore be the first item in
         any vendor conversation, not the fifth, because it determines both price and whether a deal is possible at all.

         The public-records question this audit over-penalised
         In Sierra Club v. Superior Court (Orange County), 57 Cal.4th 157 (2013), the California Supreme Court held
         that a county GIS parcel base map is a public record under the Public Records Act, that it must be produced in
         the requested format at the actual cost of duplication, and that a county may not condition its release on a
         restrictive licence. Reporting at the time noted that 47 of California’s 58 counties already published parcel
         base maps as public records.
         This does not dissolve every restriction found in this audit. It is unlikely to reach permit portals, assessor
         characteristics governed by their own statutes, or third-party layers such as the Sanborn footprints. But it
         suggests the noncommercial labels flagged on Los Gatos, Palo Alto and the San Mateo County download
         disclaimer may be weaker than a fifteen-point scoring penalty implied, at least for parcel geometry.
         Recommended action: one to two hours of California public-records counsel to scope which datasets the
         holding reaches. This is a cheap question with a potentially large answer, and it should be resolved before
         any city is written off. LedgeX should not act on this paragraph without that advice.

F / EVIDENCE ARCHITECTURE - CONTINUED

The moat, restated

The durable asset is not a scraped map or a manual report. It is the governed relationship between sources, facts, rules,
conclusions, refusals and observed outcomes.

    MOAT STATEMENT

    LedgeX organises what the record supports, where sources conflict, which conclusions depend on which inputs and how those
    conclusions affect a professional decision. Every belief is dated and reconstructable. Every unsupported conclusion is refused.
    Every source failure becomes structured operational evidence rather than an invitation to improvise.

Compounding assets

 • Versioned municipal source declarations and snapshots with per-channel rights.

 • Immutable facts, conflict sets and lineage to every source observation.

 • Deterministic rule packs and conclusion dependency manifests.

 • Golden parcels, refusal cases and measured source/schema failures.

 • Captured customer outcomes and paid behavior stored separately from property facts.

Explicit exclusions

 • Human-entered parcel facts, analyst reconciliation and portal readings in customer delivery.

 • Hidden source substitutions, unversioned rules and caveats that replace missing evidence.

 • Training or derived use of third-party data beyond its recorded licence.

    BOUNDARY

    The API, lender aggregation and land-value products are later possibilities. They require new decisions and evidence; this Audit does not
    assert that they exist.
