Data Source Checklist — City of San José v1.2
Phase 1, Step 1 covers the incorporated City of San José only. It is the Track A and Track B validation market in Business Plan
2.1.2 §14. This checklist is a controlled research and build-planning instrument; it never grants runtime eligibility and every
source must pass machine-access, rights, integration and validation gates.

    CONTROL

    Prepared August 2026. Governing sources: Business Plan 2.1.2 and Municipal Data & API Audit, Internal Strategy v1, Production
    Revision F. Engineering implementation is controlled by Spec v1.4; Implementation Rules v1.2 is its operational restatement. The
    Audit controls municipal access, licence positions and access dates.

Legend — status marks source location only
A mark in the Status column means we know where the source is and, where stated, what its licence appears to be. It does not
mean the field is integrated, normalised, validated or safe to show a professional making a real decision. See Maturity Stages
below.

 Mark         Meaning in this document

    ●         Source located, machine-readable, licence confirmed. A documented public query or bulk contract exists and the licence permits
              the intended use.

    ●         Free or public, source located, not yet integrated or licence unconfirmed. Includes portal-only and per-resource-terms datasets
              requiring confirmation at download.

    ●         Paid vendor, title company, or written permission required. No free public equivalent identified within the audit's research scope.

    ★         Derived / computed. Not sourced from any single record; produced by LedgeX from other fields and labelled as a preliminary
              conclusion.

Type column — what kind of claim the field is
The type controls presentation; source rights remain an independent gate.

•    Public record — retrieved from an official government or institutional record.

•    Third-party record — machine-retrieved from a licensed non-government source, such as MLS or vendor data; never labelled public record.

•    Estimate — modelled or algorithmic approximation, not a fact of record.

•    User assumption — request-scoped, self-reported and never inserted into the immutable fact ledger.

•    Derived conclusion — deterministic output from declared inputs; missing required inputs refuse the conclusion.

APPLY PER FIELD BEFORE TREATING IT AS PRODUCTION-READY

Maturity stages
A mark in the tables below means the source is located — nothing more. Every field must progress through these stages before it
is safe to show a user making a real decision. Stage 0 is new in this revision: it is the jurisdiction gate, and in San José it is not a
formality (see §0).

 Stage                                   Meaning

 0. Jurisdiction resolved                The parcel is confirmed to sit inside the incorporated City of San José and not in an unincorporated county
                                         pocket. Nothing downstream is valid until this passes.

 1. Source located                       We know where it exists and who the steward is.

 2. Machine-readable                     A documented REST / OGC query, open-data resource or scheduled bulk download exists (as opposed to a
                                         manual one-off lookup or a viewer without a published contract).

 3. Rights cleared                       The licence observed at retrieval permits the intended commercial use and output channel; attribution and
                                         redistribution terms are recorded. New stage. Public visibility is not commercial permission (Audit §06).

 4. Integrated                           It flows into the product with its snapshot, retrieval date and licence retained.

 5. Normalized                           It conforms to the universal parcel schema across jurisdictions.

 6. Validated                            Tested against real San José properties; discrepancies checked against a manual truth set.

 7. Build-time reviewed                  Someone confirmed the underwriting conclusion is sound on a golden parcel set — not merely that the data
                                         loaded. Internal QA at build time only. Under automated-only delivery (Blueprint v1.1 §11) no person
                                         reviews an individual customer file.

   WHY STAGE 3 WAS ADDED
   The two-county checklist tracked stage 1 only and treated rights as a downstream legal question. The audit's Sanborn finding
   demonstrated that a rights problem on a load-bearing input is an architectural gate, not a footnote. Rights clearance now sits before
   integration so that no encumbered field can silently enter the stack — the same rule the backend blueprint enforces in code.

WHAT THE ONE-CITY SCOPE ACTUALLY BUYS

Summary read — San José only
● / ● free or already sourced. Jurisdiction resolution, zoning, General Plan, overlays and Urban Villages, hazards and
environmental, entitlement and permit history, tax, contractor licensing, school boundaries, utilities, slope, and the full
local-ordinance layer. This is the bulk of the context-and-risk surface, and in San José it is unusually complete: parcels and zoning
are CC BY on a stated weekly cycle and active building permits are CC0 daily.

● must pay, or title-report territory. Rent estimates, sale comps, AVM valuation, construction cost data, school ratings, listing
status, easements and legal access. Almost entirely the pricing layer plus title-adjacent items best referred to a title company. A
small number of vendor decisions, none of which should be made before the gates clear.

●● blocked with no fallback assumed. Building footprint. Unique in this checklist: it is not an affordability problem or a
coverage problem but a rights problem with an unvalidated replacement. It is also load-bearing for most of the derived layer. If the
3DEP gate fails, roughly half the ★ rows above cannot ship.
● build and learn. Request-scoped user assumptions, separately stored customer outcome data and system-observed source failures may
accumulate outside the property-fact ledger. Manual resolution steps remain withdrawn and do not compound. No human observation or portal
reading may create a property fact.

★ derived and computed. Six named outputs, up from one in the two-county list. This is where the product becomes smart rather
than merely comprehensive — and where the refusal cascade has to work: an unmet input must propagate by name to every
conclusion that needed it.

   THE HONEST READ ON SCOPE REDUCTION
   Narrowing from thirty-five cities to one removes roughly thirty-four parallel research passes, but it removes almost none of the
   categories of work. Every layer in this checklist still has to be built. What one city buys is the ability to build each layer once,
   correctly, with its rights position recorded — and then to discover the true marginal cost of city two before committing to city
   one hundred. The two-county list described a market. This list describes a build.

Pending evidence artifacts touching this checklist
Pending artifacts apply only to rows whose stated gate they control. Parcels, zoning and active permits retain their documented licence
positions, but no source becomes runtime-active until its endpoint, schema, terms and snapshot are verified in the source ledger.

• San José licence confirmations — per-resource clearance for the inspection and planning permit series.
• 3DEP extraction benchmark — coverage map, method and repeatable workflow over a defined San José test area.
• 3DEP accuracy result — measured error against known structures, with approved error bars.
• Source and licence ledger — URL, layer or item ID, raw snapshot, retrieval date and observed terms for every launch source.
• Public-records counsel note — scope of Sierra Club v. Superior Court and the commercial-use posture of each launch
  source.
• Eligible-parcel-demand denominator — a defined eligible-parcel universe for San José. Permit activity is observed activity,
  not a demand rate.
